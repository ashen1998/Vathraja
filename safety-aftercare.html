Safety and suitability

Safe Facial Care Starts with the Right Assessment

Suitability is a clinical decision. Before any treatment at Vathraja Facial Chambers, your clinician reviews your skin, your history, and your expectations — and will tell you plainly when something is not appropriate for you.

Suitability

What we check before agreeing to treat

Every one of these can change what is appropriate — or mean that nothing should be done today.

Medical history

Current and past conditions, including anything affecting healing, immunity, or bleeding.

Medications and supplements

Prescribed and over-the-counter, including recent courses that may still be relevant.

Allergies and sensitivities

Known allergies, previous reactions to products or procedures, and sensitivity patterns.

Pregnancy and breastfeeding

Several treatments are deferred during pregnancy and breastfeeding as a matter of caution.

Recent treatments and sun exposure

Recent procedures, active exfoliants, or significant sun exposure may mean postponing.

Skin condition on the day

Active infection, broken skin, or an irritated barrier takes priority over any planned treatment.

Aftercare

What to do after a facial aesthetic treatment

Aftercare is where a good result is protected. You will receive written guidance specific to your treatment; these are the principles that apply across most of them.

If something does not feel right, contact the clinic rather than waiting for your next appointment. Unexpected irritation, increasing pain, spreading redness, or any symptom that concerns you should be reviewed.

General aftercare guidance

  • Follow the recommended post-treatment routine you were given
  • Avoid harsh or active products unless you have been advised otherwise
  • Use sun protection, especially after brightening or exfoliating treatments
  • Contact the clinic if you experience unexpected irritation or discomfort
  • Attend follow-up appointments where these have been recommended

Declining and deferring

When we recommend not proceeding

Part of specialist care is being willing to say no. We may recommend deferring or declining treatment when:

  • Your expectations could not realistically be met by the treatment discussed
  • Your skin is currently irritated, infected, or recovering from another procedure
  • A medical condition or medication makes the procedure inadvisable
  • The timing is too close to an important event to allow for settling and review
  • A different pathway — or a referral outside our scope — would serve you better
