What we review

Refreshed, not altered

The aim is that people notice you look well, not that you have had something done. That outcome starts with what we look at before anything is proposed.

Facial proportion and symmetry

How features relate to one another, and whether a concern is a feature in isolation or a question of overall balance.

Skin quality

Texture, tone, hydration, and laxity. Skin quality often explains more about a “tired” appearance than volume does.

Expression and movement

A face in motion, not only at rest — because a natural-looking result has to hold up when you talk and smile.

Your goals and reference points

What you would like to change, in your words, and whether that goal is achievable with what is available.

Previous treatments

What has been done before, when, and how your skin responded. This changes what is appropriate now.

Timelines

How long a result may take to appear and settle, particularly where an event or milestone is involved.

Treatment approach

Planned as a whole face, and paced

Facial aesthetics at Vathraja is planned by a consultant or doctor who assesses the face as a whole. Where treatment is appropriate, we usually prefer a measured approach: a conservative starting point, a review, and an adjustment if one is needed.

That pacing is deliberate. It is easier to build gradually towards a result you are happy with than to reverse one that has gone too far.

What a plan sets out

  • The concern, described in clinical terms and in yours
  • Which approaches may support that concern, and which will not
  • A conservative starting point where appropriate
  • Expected onset, duration, and what maintenance would involve
  • Risks, side effects, and what to watch for afterwards
  • A review point before anything further is considered

A note on safety and suitability

Facial aesthetic treatments are medical procedures. Suitability depends on your anatomy, medical history, medications, and expectations, and is confirmed only after clinical assessment.

We will decline to treat where a request is not clinically appropriate, where expectations cannot realistically be met, or where waiting would serve you better. Saying so is part of specialist care.
